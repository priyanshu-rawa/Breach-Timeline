ABOUT THIS PROJECT

Why I built this

I built Breach Timeline because I kept finding cybersecurity history scattered across dozens of articles, reports, and blog posts — never in one place you could actually explore. This site is my attempt to fix that: a single, browsable timeline of the attacks that shaped how we think about digital security, from the Morris Worm in 1988 to what's happening in 2026. It's also a small personal project to combine two things I enjoy — learning how these attacks actually worked, and building things for the web.

STAY SAFE

Helpful tips before you go

Use a password manager

Long, unique passwords for every account beat memorized ones every time. A single reused password is how most account breaches start.

Turn on multi-factor authentication

MFA is the single biggest thing you can do to stop a stolen password from becoming a compromised account. Enable it everywhere it's offered.

Keep everything updated

Most major attacks in this timeline exploited a bug that already had a patch available. Turn on automatic updates for your OS, browser, and apps.

Slow down on links and attachments

Phishing is still behind the majority of breaches. If a message creates urgency or asks you to click something unexpected, pause before acting.

Back up important files

Keep an offline or cloud backup separate from your main device. It's the one thing that makes ransomware a nuisance instead of a disaster.

Be careful on public Wi-Fi

Avoid logging into sensitive accounts on open networks, or use a trusted VPN if you have to. Public Wi-Fi is easy to eavesdrop on.
